# Business Model

## 1. Business Overview

We are building a vertical SaaS platform for women's beauty salons.

The platform combines:

- Salon CRM
- Customer Intelligence
- Retention & Revenue Automation
- Marketing Automation
- B2B Procurement & Marketplace
- Optional Employee Attribution & Incentive Management

The product is designed to become the **operating intelligence layer of a beauty salon**, rather than simply another salon management system.

---

# 2. Core Business Problem

Beauty salons generate large amounts of customer, service, employee and purchasing data, but this data is often:

- fragmented
- poorly structured
- operational rather than analytical
- not converted into actionable insights
- disconnected from marketing
- disconnected from procurement
- disconnected from employee incentives

Existing generic CRM systems are not sufficiently specialized around the economics and workflows of beauty salons.

The opportunity is to build a system that understands the specific behavior of salon customers and converts operational data into revenue-generating actions.

# 7. Strategic Product Wedges

## Wedge 1 — Retention & Revenue Intelligence

Primary wedge.

The system analyzes customer behavior and identifies revenue opportunities.

Core loop:

```text
Customer Data
→ Behavioral Understanding
→ Opportunity Detection
→ Recommended Action
→ Customer Action
→ Revenue
→ Measurement
```

The key commercial promise is:

> We help salons generate incremental revenue from their existing customer base.

---

## Wedge 2 — B2B Beauty Supply Marketplace

Second growth wedge.

The platform connects salons with suppliers of professional beauty products and consumables.

Core loop:

```text
Salon Consumption
→ Supply Need
→ Product Discovery
→ Supplier Discovery
→ Price Comparison
→ Procurement
```

Potential revenue mechanisms:

- Supplier commissions
- Transaction fees
- Marketplace fees
- Supplier subscriptions
- Premium procurement services
- Sponsored supplier placement, subject to trust and transparency

The marketplace should become stronger as salon purchasing data increases.

---

## Wedge 3 — Attribution & Incentive Engine

Optional third wedge.

This capability allows selected salons to create business-specific employee attribution and incentive rules.

Example:

```text
Employee A acquires customer
→ Customer purchases another service
→ Attribution rule applies
→ Revenue is attributed
→ Employee reward is calculated
```

This should not be required by every salon.

Commercially, it can become:

- Premium module
- Enterprise capability
- Usage-based feature
- Custom configuration

Architecturally, it must remain optional.

# 16. Strategic Principle

The company should optimize for:

> **Increasing the economic value of every salon relationship.**

Every major product initiative should answer:

1. Does it increase customer value?
2. Does it increase salon revenue?
3. Does it reduce salon cost?
4. Does it increase product adoption?
5. Does it create a defensible data or network advantage?

If none apply, the feature should be questioned.

---

# 17. Business Model Assumptions

The following are hypotheses, not facts:

- Salons will pay recurring SaaS fees for measurable business value.
- Retention intelligence is a stronger initial wedge than procurement.
- Existing customer data can be converted into actionable revenue opportunities.
- Salons will trust the platform with important customer data if the product demonstrates clear value.
- Procurement can become a meaningful second monetization layer.
- Attribution and incentive functionality will be valuable to a subset of salons.
- The combination of CRM + intelligence + marketplace can produce stronger retention than standalone salon software.

These assumptions must eventually be validated through customer discovery, pilots and product data.

---

# 18. Business Validation Principle

Do not confuse:

```text
Feature Built
```

with:

```text
Business Validated
```

A business hypothesis is validated through evidence:

```text
Customer Problem
→ Product Adoption
→ User Behavior
→ Measurable Outcome
→ Willingness to Pay
```

The roadmap should prioritize learning and validation over feature volume.
